# HOUSE STYLE
### Standing Orders for the Writing of Documents That People Actually Read (Rev. 1, 26 Oct 2026)

> *This document is extremely serious. It is also the reason the other documents are fun.
> Both of those are true, and that is the whole style.*

---

## §0. Origin

On 26 October 2026, `PIMP-OUT-YOUR-FLOW.md` was read in full by its commissioner, who reported that he "followed it", "had a damn good time", and "loved every line", and then issued the following standing order, quoted here in full and in good faith:

> "This needs to be our new gold standard for communication outside ... a cool signature that we leave in our project."

This document codifies that signature so it can be left on purpose.

## §1. Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **House Style** | Documentation written as a deadpan official procedure, in which every command is real and every joke is also true. |
| **The Frame** | The mock-official wrapper: title, revision, preamble, numbered §sections. The frame is the joke. |
| **The Facts** | Commands, numbers, rules, receipts. The facts are never the joke. |
| **The Signature** | The closing statement and the committee line. A House Style document without one is unsigned and therefore suspicious. |

## §2. Required Elements

Every House Style document SHALL contain:

1. **A title in capitals** and a subtitle that sounds like a government form ("Official Procedures for…", "Standing Orders for…").
2. **A revision and date** (`Rev. 1, 26 Oct 2026`). Documents improve; revisions admit it.
3. **The preamble:** one italic line declaring the document extremely serious, in a way that tells the reader they are allowed to smile.
4. **Numbered §sections.** §0 is for history and whereases.
5. **A definitions table** that defines at least one word nobody thought needed defining.
6. **Commands that run.** Every code block was executed before the document shipped. A command that does not run is not a joke. It is a bug.
7. **Real receipts:** dates, counts, timings and the actual bug the procedure caught. The truth is funnier than anything we could invent, and it has the advantage of being true.
8. **A "Prohibited" section**, where the real rules live in plain words: disclosure of AI echoes, consent and takedowns, never re-uploading other people's media, never claiming a device works because a mock said so. The surrounding tone may be playful. These sentences may not.
9. **A closing statement** that says, in one or two sentences, what "done" looks like, and then admits that we'll do it again anyway.
10. **The Committee line**, crediting who made it. Kurtis is credited by name.

## §3. Permitted Escalations

- **Ambition levels** (Mild / Medium / Fully Pimped / Ridiculous) for any procedure with optional depth.
- **Whereas clauses**, sparingly. Three is a rhythm; five is a filibuster.
- **Ratings with receipts** ("a rating without a receipt is a rumour").
- **Callbacks** to earlier documents, which reward the reader who read them.

## §4. Prohibited Writing

- Jokes about the facts. The frame may wink. The numbers may not.
- Making disclosure, consent or safety sound optional, ironic, or like a bit.
- Invented flavour: fake quotes, fake numbers, fake history. If it didn't happen, it doesn't go in.
- Using House Style where a second matters: an incident runbook or a recovery procedure. Those are written plain, then signed.
- Burying the command. The reader came for the command. Give them the command.

## §5. Where It Applies

| Document | Style |
|---|---|
| README, AGENTS.md, how-tos, procedures, project docs for other people or agents | **House Style** |
| ETHICS.md | Plain words in the body; the House Style frame is allowed around it |
| Incident runbooks, recovery steps | Plain, with the signature only |
| Chat, Telegram, status updates | Not documents. Plain. |

## §6. Closing Statement

A House Style document is finished when a stranger reads it to the end, runs every command, follows every rule, and comes away having had a better time than documentation has any right to give.

Then it gets a new revision.
