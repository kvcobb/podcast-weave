Podcast Weave

Somebody on a podcast says something interesting and you want to ask one more question. A weave lets you: the picture freezes, AI echoes of the people in the room keep the conversation going, and then the episode picks up from the exact second you left, from the creator's own upload, as if nobody had touched anything.

Nobody touched anything. The original is never copied or re-uploaded. Its views and its ads stay with its creator. The only videos that are ours are the pauses.

What's real and what isn't

Everything in the pause segments is AI-generated. Every voice there is an AI echo, not the real person, and none of it was said by them. Each pause says so on screen and names the model behind the echoes, and each video carries YouTube's synthetic-content label.

Harmony and Joscha are AI seats. Joscha's voice is borrowed from public recordings of Joscha Bach, who is not speaking. The Kurtis who stops the clock is an AI echo of Kurtis, in a voice cloned from his own recordings.

If you're one of the people echoed and want a weave taken down, open an issue on this repository and it comes down.

Make your own

Who it's for: anyone with a coding agent (Claude Code, Codex, or similar) and a little patience. What it costs: on the order of 100 to 150 language-model calls per pause, plus voices: free and local on a decent GPU (the OmniVoice weights are non-commercial, CC-BY-NC-4.0), on a GPU box you rent, or ElevenLabs, which is paid. Watching costs nothing.

Bring a YouTube episode, the moment you want to step in, a few minutes of yourself talking about the subject, a short clean clip of your voice, and a photo. Then:

  1. Hand the repository to your coding agent and point it at AGENTS.md. It checks your machine first with python -m cpc doctor, then walks you through the rest.
  2. One pause: python -m cpc ingest, souls, me, voices, pause, render, companion, weave.
  3. Several pauses on several models: python -m cpc grid init, then grid next, one pause at a time. Read each room before you accept it. Every room. Yes, that one too.
  4. Before you show anyone: python -m cpc.flowlab walks every route through your weave and listens at every switch, so you find the stutter before your viewers do.

Read ETHICS.md before you share anything. This is a shared method, not a product. If you make an echo video, you're responsible for it, and nothing here is legal advice or a promise that a video is lawful.
